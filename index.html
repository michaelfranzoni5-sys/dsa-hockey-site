The DSA System

50 off-ice drills across 5 categories. Build the hands, shot, and power to level up.

The DSA System is a full off-ice training program designed for hockey players who want to train seriously at home. Every drill has a setup, instructions, coaching cues, and rep scheme — so you know exactly what to do and how to do it right.

🏒

Stickhandling

19 drills

Warm-up through advanced. Build soft hands, puck control, and the ability to handle pressure in tight spaces.

Sample: Toe Drags → Shot — 3 pucks lined up. Toe drag each one into the next, then release. Teaches smooth transitions from deke to finish.
🎯

Shooting

13 drills

Game-specific finishing. Quick release, one-timers, backhands, drag shots, and breakaway moves.

Sample: Pass Under the Net/Chair — pass under a "defender," step around, and bury the rebound. Teaches shooting in traffic.
📤

Passing

3 drills

Build precision, soft hands, and the ability to move the puck under pressure.

Sample: Sauce with Tire — saucer pass the puck over a tire to the net. Adjust lift until it clears and lands flat.
💪

Workout

6 exercises

Hockey-specific conditioning — explosive power, core stability, and endurance that translates to the ice.

Sample: McDavid Drill — 10-yard sprint, touch ground, sprint back, sprint 20. Repeat. Builds explosive acceleration and change of direction.
⚡

Plyometrics

9 drills

Explosive first step, lateral quickness, and agility that separate fast players from slow ones.

Sample: 5-10-5 Shuffle into Explosion — shuffle 5 yards, 10 yards the other way, back to center, then explode into a 10-yard sprint.

What You'll Need

Stick, pucks (or golf balls for tight spaces), 3 tires or cones, a chair, a net or garage door with a target, and space to move. Nothing crazy — most of this you already have.

FAQ

What kind of clips should I send?

Any off-ice training — shooting, stickhandling, edge work, weight transfer drills. 30–60 seconds is ideal. Record horizontally. Slow-motion on your phone is helpful but not required.

How is this different from a regular coach?

On-ice coaches split attention across 10–20 kids. They miss details. I look at your clip frame-by-frame with nothing else to focus on. The drills I give you are built around what I see in YOUR video, not a generic template.

Is this for younger players too?

Absolutely. The best time to fix form is early. If a 9-year-old learns proper weight transfer and hand positioning now, they'll have a much harder shot at 14 than someone who picked up bad habits. I work with all ages.
